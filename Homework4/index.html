Penetration Process: Law of Large Numbers and Central Limit Theorem

A simulation study of penetration-score trajectories, the Law of Large Numbers, and the Central Limit Theorem.

01Model and interpretation

We consider n systems and m independent attackers. Each attacker attempts one step per system. The score increment at step i is an independent, identically distributed random variable Xᵢ. A trajectory shows the cumulative penetration score after each system.

Bernoulli modelXᵢ ∈ {0, 1}; P(Xᵢ = 1) = pSuccess adds 1, failure adds 0. E[X] = p; Var(X) = p(1 − p).
Rademacher modelXᵢ ∈ {−1, +1}; P(+1) = P(−1) = ½Positive and negative steps balance. E[X] = 0; Var(X) = 1.

Assumptions: steps are i.i.d. within each attacker; attackers are independent; all attackers face the same model. This is a simplified score model, not a realistic security risk forecast.

Here μ = E[Xᵢ] and σ² = Var(Xᵢ). For Bernoulli, μ = p and σ² = p(1−p). For Rademacher, μ = 0 and σ² = 1.

Important distinction: The uncentered Sₙ/√n converges to N(0,1) in the Rademacher model. In Bernoulli with p > 0, its mean grows as p√n, so it does not converge to a fixed standard normal. The centered and standardized Bernoulli score (Sₙ−np)/√(np(1−p)) converges to N(0,1) when 0 < p < 1.

04Law of Large Numbers (LLN)

For i.i.d. increments with finite mean, the sample average converges in probability to μ as n increases:

P(|Sₙ/n − μ| > ε) → 0 for every ε > 0.

The average has variance σ²/n, which decreases toward zero. Chebyshev’s inequality gives P(|Sₙ/n − μ| > ε) ≤ σ²/(nε²). The experiment below compares empirical deviations with this upper bound.

05Central Limit Theorem (CLT)

In the symmetric Rademacher case, the increments have mean zero and variance one. Thus the normalized sum converges in distribution to a standard normal:

Sₙ/√n ⇒ N(0, 1) as n → ∞.

07Discussion: systems and attackers

Effect of n (systems): Each attacker performs more independent steps. In the Bernoulli model, the expected total increases linearly as np; in the Rademacher model, the expected total remains zero while its standard deviation grows as √n. In either case, the average becomes more stable as n increases.

Effect of m (attackers): More attackers provide more independent trajectories and a more reliable estimate of the score distribution. Changing m does not change the theoretical distribution of any individual attacker's score. If the scores of all attackers are summed, the expected aggregate is mnμ and its variance is mnσ² under independence.

LLN: Sₙ/n approaches p for Bernoulli and 0 for Rademacher, with variance decreasing as 1/n. CLT: Sₙ/√n approaches N(0,1) in distribution for Rademacher, despite the discrete ±1 increments. Normalization: Dividing by n stabilizes the average; dividing by √n stabilizes variance, but a nonzero mean must be centered before applying a standard-normal CLT.

Limitations: Real vulnerabilities differ between systems, and attacks may share tools or information. Correlated increments can change the variance and invalidate the independent-increment scaling used here.

Takeaway: Increasing the number of systems changes the scale and concentration of the penetration score; increasing the number of attackers improves the empirical visibility of its distribution. The Bernoulli model represents accumulating successes, while the Rademacher model isolates symmetric fluctuations around zero.
